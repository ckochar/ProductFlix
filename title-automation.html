01 · THE PROBLEM

The work looked like one process.
It was actually a chain of manual handoffs.

Title search and production crossed external sources, documents, internal systems and people. Every manual touch increased cycle time, inconsistency and operating cost.

01

Manual Search

Operators navigated provider or title-source workflows, entered search criteria and retrieved results manually.

02

Manual Keying

Relevant information from reports and documents had to be interpreted and entered into downstream systems.

03

Manual Assembly

Documents, data and production outputs were combined into the final package through multiple repetitive steps.

04

Fragmented Exceptions

When data was missing or ambiguous, work depended on individual judgment rather than a consistent exception model.

05

Different LOB Rules

Eight business lines shared a core problem but had different products, providers, rules and downstream needs.

06

Hard-to-Scale Labor

Growth meant adding operational capacity unless the workflow itself changed.

02 · PRODUCT STRATEGY

Don’t automate a task.
Automate the production system.

The product bet was to treat title production as an orchestrated workflow: rules decide what to search, integrations retrieve it, automation interprets it, and people resolve only the uncertain cases.

1. Configure, don’t hard-code

Shared platform capabilities with business-line-specific rules allow the same automation backbone to support multiple LOBs.

2. Integrate at the source

Title plants and data providers become part of the workflow rather than destinations users manually visit.

3. Remove keying

Extract structured data from retrieved documents and source outputs, validate it, and write it downstream automatically.

4. Assemble automatically

Retrieve the required evidence and documents, apply package rules, and build the production output without repetitive operator work.

5. Design exceptions first

Ambiguity, missing data and provider failures route to a human queue with context instead of breaking the automated flow.

6. Preserve control

Automation needs source traceability, confidence, auditability and human override because the output supports regulated workflows.

04 · KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS

The hard part wasn’t OCR.
It was designing the operating model around automation.

The product choices determine whether automation becomes a scalable platform or a brittle collection of scripts.

Shared platform vs. eight bespoke automations

Build reusable orchestration and configuration primitives, then express LOB variation as rules and profiles.

Rules vs. AI

Use deterministic logic where the answer is knowable; use extraction/intelligence for unstructured content; never ask a model to solve what a rule can settle.

Happy path vs. exception path

Success is not “100% touchless.” Success is maximizing safe straight-through processing while making exceptions faster to resolve.

Provider abstraction

Keep downstream workflows from being tightly coupled to one external source so providers can evolve without redesigning the product.

Source traceability

Every automated field needs an explainable source so reviewers can verify why the system populated it.

Scale through observability

Track where automation succeeds, where it falls out, why it falls out, and which rules/providers drive operational load.
