What if document QC happened before humans had to find the errors?

01 · THE PROBLEM

High-volume document verification was still a human search problem.

Reviewers repeatedly inspected document packages for execution, completeness and consistency issues. Many checks were predictable, but people still had to locate evidence manually before making a decision.

The product opportunity was not simply to “add AI.” It was to determine which decisions could be automated deterministically, which required interpretation, and which should remain human-owned.

02 · DISCOVERY

The breakthrough wasn't “use AI.” It was finding where AI was unnecessary.

Working with operations, I decomposed the verification workflow into repeatable decision types. The key insight was that many conditions did not need AI at all.

That changed the solution architecture. Instead of asking a model to judge everything, deterministic validation could remain explicit and auditable while AI handled variable document interpretation and ambiguity.

04 · THE PRODUCT

AI reviews first. Humans review what matters.

Document Verification creates a first-pass QC layer before manual review. It ingests a package, identifies relevant documents and execution evidence, runs deterministic checks, applies AI interpretation where needed, and routes exceptions with evidence for human confirmation.

05 · KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS

The product is defined by the boundaries.

01

Why not automate 100%?

In regulated workflows, false confidence can be more expensive than manual effort. Critical or ambiguous conditions remain reviewable by design.

02

Why rules before LLMs?

If a condition is deterministic, making it probabilistic adds cost and risk without adding product value.

03

Why evidence with every exception?

An auditor should not have to trust a model. They should see exactly what triggered the decision and where the relevant evidence lives.

04

Why phased rollout?

Start with more structured workflows, validate accuracy and exception behavior, then expand into higher-variation scenarios.

06 · ROLLOUT

Roll out by uncertainty, not by ambition.

I favored a staged rollout that began with more structured workflows and progressively introduced higher-variation scenarios. Each phase was intended to validate accuracy, exception behavior and reviewer trust before expanding automation coverage.

01Structured flowsValidate accuracy and evidence
02Broader variationExpand document and exception diversity
03Higher ambiguityScale only after trust is established

08 · WHAT I’D DO NEXT
01

Build an evaluation harness

Track precision, recall, critical false negatives and reviewer disagreement by rule and document type.

02

Turn overrides into learning

Use human corrections to identify weak rules, ambiguous layouts and model failure modes.

03

Add cross-document intelligence

Move from validating pages independently to identifying contradictions across the entire package.

04

Scale profiles by workflow

Make validation policies configurable by channel, client, document package and risk tolerance.
