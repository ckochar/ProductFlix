THE PRODUCT PROBLEM

Checkout friction was not just a UX issue. It was a revenue issue.

Customers could reach checkout with strong purchase intent and still drop because payment states, failure handling and recovery paths introduced uncertainty. The opportunity was to simplify the journey while improving the reliability of the transaction underneath it.

01 · CONVERSION

Reduce abandonment

Remove avoidable friction from the final steps of the reservation journey and make required actions easier to understand.

02 · RELIABILITY

Design for payment states

Treat authorization, decline, retry and confirmation states as first-class product experiences rather than technical edge cases.

03 · RECOVERY

Preserve customer intent

When a payment fails, help the traveler recover without forcing them to reconstruct the entire reservation.

HOW I APPROACHED IT

Optimize the transaction system, not only the checkout screen.

“A failed authorization is a product moment before it becomes a support ticket.”
  • Map the end-to-end funnel: identify where intent was lost between cart, payment and confirmation.
  • Separate user friction from transaction failure: diagnose experience problems and payment-system problems differently.
  • Make recovery explicit: give customers clear next actions when a payment or downstream state changes.
  • Use business outcomes: measure successful bookings and abandonment, not only interface engagement.
